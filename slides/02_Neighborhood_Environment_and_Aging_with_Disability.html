Slide 2: Aging with disability

Diagram: Diagram of three points about aging with disability: the average age of people living with disability-associated conditions is increasing; conditions are acquired at birth, in childhood, or before midlife; and people can experience premature aging. Premature aging branches into two outcomes: medical conditions such as hypertension, obesity, heart disease, and diabetes are more prevalent in people aging with disability, and secondary health conditions such as bone loss, fractures, rotator cuff injuries, and pain.

Source: Molton and Ordway, 2019

Slide 12: Implications

Slide 13: Recreational facilities

Image: An empty indoor fitness center with weight machines, a punching bag, and cardio equipment by the windows

Living in a neighborhood with a greater density of recreational facilities is associated with:

11%

reduced risk of cardiometabolic disease in adults aging with disability

Critical that recreational facilities are accessible and welcoming to people as they age with disability

Slide 14: Grocery stores

Image: A woman using a wheelchair reaches up to a high shelf in the dairy aisle of a grocery store

Living in a neighborhood with a greater density of grocery stores is associated with:

5%

increased risk of cardiometabolic disease in adults aging with disability

Healthy food in grocery stores is often more expensive;

Barriers in grocery stores (narrow aisles, tall shelves) may deter shopping

Slide 15: Traffic

Image: An older adult with white hair, seen from behind, rides a power wheelchair along a tree-lined path

Living in a neighborhood with lower traffic is associated with:

15%

reduced risk of cardiometabolic disease in adults aging with disability

People with disability often rely on public transit or pedestrian walkways, and may not have resources to own/adapt a vehicle

Heavy traffic is the top environmental factor in injuries and deaths among disabled pedestrians (Schwartz, 2022)
