Slide 5: Census tracts and ZCTAs

Census tract

ZCTA (ZIP Code Tabulation Area)

A small, stable statistical area, usually 1,200 to 8,000 people.

The Census Bureau's area version of a ZIP code, built from census blocks.

Nests inside a county, the way counties nest inside a state.

Not the boundary the Postal Service uses, and not nested inside anything.

Most NaNDA measures are available at this level.

ZIP ≠ ZCTA

A ZIP code is a mail delivery route. A ZCTA is a statistical area. They are not interchangeable. ZIP codes need a crosswalk to ZCTAs (Intermediate Session).

Slide 14: NaNDA location variables

VARIABLE

WHAT IT IS

tract_fips10

Census tract, 2010 boundaries (11 digits)

tract_fips20

Census tract, 2020 boundaries (11 digits)

└ tract_fips22

The same 2020 tracts, recoded for Connecticut's 2022 boundary change. An additional variable in the 2020 files, not a separate dataset. Use it if your data were geocoded after 2022 and include Connecticut.

zcta10

ZCTA, 2010 boundaries (5 digits)

zcta20

ZCTA, 2020 boundaries (5 digits)

The suffix tells you the geographic boundaries. Recent NaNDA datasets use these variable names; older ones may differ, so check the documentation.

A yearly dataset has a value for every tract in every year, so each interview is matched to its own year. A 2016 interview gets the 2016 value for that tract; a 2019 interview gets the 2019 value.

match the year.

Slide 22: One year, or every year?

Slide 29: Where and When, so far

Where?

When?

  • Census tracts nest within counties. ZCTAs nest in nothing.
  • ZIP ≠ ZCTA. ZIP codes need a crosswalk first.
  • Boundaries follow the geocoding date: before 2020 means 2010.
  • Yearly datasets: match the interview year.
  • Period datasets: pick the window that overlaps most, and say so.
  • Boundaries first, then years. A year or two of gap is fine.

Jordan: Civic 2016 to 2019, every year. SES 2013 to 2017, gap noted in methods.
